## One “to be”, two verbs

If you're learning Spanish, you've probably already hit this wall: why does Spanish need two verbs for the one English “to be”? Telling **ser** and **estar** apart is one of the first real hurdles for every learner — and one of the most satisfying to get past.

## When do we use ser?

**Ser** is for what something *is*:

- **Identity**: Soy Ana. (I'm Ana.)
- **Origin**: Somos de Serbia. (We're from Serbia.)
- **Profession**: Es profesora. (She's a teacher.)
- **Lasting traits**: La casa es grande. (The house is big.)
- **Time and date**: Son las tres. (It's three o'clock.)

## When do we use estar?

**Estar** is for how or where something *is*:

- **Location**: Estoy en Madrid. (I'm in Madrid.)
- **Temporary states**: Estoy cansado. (I'm tired.)
- **Feelings**: Está contenta. (She's happy.)
- **Result of an action**: La puerta está abierta. (The door is open.)

## A trick to remember it

A simple rule that works at the start: if something can easily change, use **estar**. If it's a lasting characteristic, use **ser**.

There are exceptions and nuances you'll pick up with time — a few of them are in the questions below — but this rule covers most everyday situations. ¡Buena suerte con el español!
