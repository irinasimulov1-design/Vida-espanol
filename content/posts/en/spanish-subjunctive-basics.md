## What is the subjunctive?

The subjunctive (el subjuntivo) is a verb mood that expresses wishes, doubts, emotions and situations that aren't (yet) real. English barely uses it any more — think of “I wish he *were* here” — which is exactly why it feels so unfamiliar.

While the indicative describes the world as it is, the subjunctive describes the world as we'd like it to be, or situations we aren't sure about.

## When is it used?

There are four main situations where you'll need the subjunctive:

### 1. Wishes and requests

When you say what you want someone else to do:

- **Quiero que vengas** — I want you to come
- **Espero que estés bien** — I hope you're well
- **Te pido que me ayudes** — I'm asking you to help me

### 2. Emotions

When you say how you feel about something:

- **Me alegra que aprendas español** — I'm glad you're learning Spanish
- **Siento que no puedas venir** — I'm sorry you can't come

### 3. Doubt and uncertainty

When you're not sure whether something is true:

- **No creo que sea fácil** — I don't think it's easy
- **Dudo que tenga razón** — I doubt he's right

### 4. Impersonal expressions

- **Es importante que practiques** — It's important that you practise
- **Es posible que llueva** — It might rain

## How do you form it?

The trick is simple — do the opposite of what you usually do:

- **-ar** verbs take endings with **e**: hable, hables, hable, hablemos, habléis, hablen
- **-er/-ir** verbs take endings with **a**: coma, comas, coma, comamos, comáis, coman

## How to practise

The best way to master the subjunctive is through context. Instead of memorising rules, try to:

- Listen to songs in Spanish and spot the subjunctive
- Read short stories and highlight the verbs in the subjunctive
- Write one sentence with the subjunctive every day

The subjunctive takes patience, but once it clicks, your Spanish will sound much more natural. ¡Ánimo!
